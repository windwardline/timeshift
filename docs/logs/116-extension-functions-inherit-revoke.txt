=== Does the database-wide FUNCTIONS revoke reach extension functions? Measured. ===

20260819001500 block 2 guards its sweep of EXISTING functions against
extension members, and says why: revoking EXECUTE from PUBLIC on pgcrypto
and friends would break every other role that calls them. Block 1 -- the
default-privileges revoke -- admits no such carve-out, because the
schema-qualified spelling that could target one schema is the spelling that
does nothing. So: do extension functions created LATER inherit it?

On the Supabase-shaped database from log 112, with all migrations applied:
  CREATE EXTENSION pgcrypto;
 proname  |                    proacl                     | anon | service_role | owner 
----------+-----------------------------------------------+------+--------------+-------
 crypt    | {postgres=X/postgres,service_role=X/postgres} | f    | t            | t
 digest   | {postgres=X/postgres,service_role=X/postgres} | f    | t            | t
 digest   | {postgres=X/postgres,service_role=X/postgres} | f    | t            | t
 gen_salt | {postgres=X/postgres,service_role=X/postgres} | f    | t            | t
(4 rows)

Yes -- they inherit it. The owner and service_role keep EXECUTE; anon and
authenticated do not.

For this app that is the wanted direction: nothing here calls PostgREST, the
app connects as the owner, and an extension function reachable by anon is
precisely the RPC surface this migration closes. Recorded because it is a
real consequence with an unbounded window -- an operator enabling an
extension from the dashboard months from now would otherwise see
'permission denied for function ...' with nothing pointing back here. The
remedy is an explicit GRANT EXECUTE on the functions they mean to publish,
not reopening the default.
